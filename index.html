About me

Curious by default.
Practical by design.

I’m an Egyptian-Canadian engineer based in Milton, Ontario. I started in biomedical engineering at Cairo University, where medical imaging, signals, software, and healthcare operations first came together for me. Today, I’m studying AI Development & Applications at Sheridan College and building products that connect intelligent models with dependable systems.

My professional path has crossed telecom infrastructure, automotive LiDAR delivery, hospital imaging support, national-scale device data, and applied AI. That range taught me to think beyond the model: data quality, deployment, observability, documentation, and the operator experience all determine whether technology is genuinely useful.

I’m happiest turning a research idea into something tangible—an automation that saves a team hours, an interface that clarifies noisy data, or an ML pipeline that keeps working after the demo. Outside engineering, I practice drums, explore digital art, read research and fiction, and stay curious about emerging areas such as quantum AI.
